America's Hidden Hunger

In the U.S., millions of children don't know where their next meal is coming from. This is a problem occurring right now in communities across our nation. This article offer a clearer picture of hunger in America and explains how we're capable of making meaningful progress.

Whether you're a policymaker shaping nutrition programs, an advocate working in education or social services, or a nonprofit leader fighting hunger in your community, this story is for you. You'll see not just the scale of the crisis, but where it's happening, why it's happening and what solutions already exist that aren't reaching those who need them most.
The Reality Check
Before we begin, take a moment to consider how many in 10 kids face food insecurity? Make your guess. The reality might surprise you.

Food insecurity in children is defined as the limited or uncertain access to adequate food.
2 in 10 Children Go Hungry!
This is over 13 million kids across America who don't know where their next meal is coming from. To put that in perspective, imagine filling every NFL stadium in the country to capacity, then doing it 130 more times. Behind this statistic are real children facing impossible circumstances every day. Let's explore where and why this is happening.

Participation Gaps Across States
Even when programs exist to help, not everyone who qualifies takes part. This chart shows the gap between those eligible for food aid and those actually participating, state by state. Each line connects 100% eligibility (gray) to the actual participation rate (green).

The story becomes clear: while some states manage to reach most of their eligible residents, others leave many behind. Look closely and you'll see where outreach is working, and where there's room for improvement. This is more than numbers; it's about real people who could benefit from support but aren't receiving it.
Hungry Hinders Learning
Imagine a classroom of ten students. Five sit ready to learn because they received a free or reduced-price school breakfast. The other five didn't, and studies say it shows.

Across the country, 50 million children are eligible for school breakfast, but only half participate. In this visualization, green students represent those who ate; red students represent those who didn't.

The impact is immediate: hungry students struggle to focus, keep up, and stay engaged. A simple meal can be the difference between starting the day ready to learn, or starting already behind.
Income Makes All the Difference
Children living in households below 185% of the federal poverty level experience significantly higher rates of food insecurity than those above it. This cutoff is commonly used to determine eligibility for federal nutrition programs like free and reduced-price school meals and WIC.

Even when families qualify for food assistance, food insecurity often remains high, exposing the distance between policy on paper and life in practice. Eligibility does not always translate into steady participation, adequate benefits, or full coverage of food needs. And while food insecurity declines above the 185% threshold, it does not vanish, leaving some families caught both inside and just outside the safety net.

What You Can Do

Hunger touches every community in the U.S. Here are some things that can help ensure kids and families have the food they need

Volunteer: Lend a few hours at a local food bank or pantry to sort, pack, or distribute food. Find a food bank near you.

Donate: One-time or monthly gifts help keep shelves stocked and trucks on the road. Learn how your gift helps.

Advocate: Add your voice to protect and strengthen programs like SNAP and school meals. Take action.
