The Reality Check
Before we begin, take a moment to consider how many in 10 kids face food insecurity? Make your guess. The reality might surprise you.
2 in 10 Children Go Hungry!
This is over 13 million kids across America who don't know where their next meal is coming from. To put that in perspective, imagine filling every NFL stadium in the country to capacity, then doing it 130 more times. Behind this statistic are real children facing impossible circumstances every day. Let's explore where and why this is happening.

State Level Food Insecurity Comparison (2023)
Each state outline sits on a number line (13% to 24%) at that state’s child food-insecurity rate. Hover to see the exact percent and the name of the state. The color gradient is from lower (lighter blue color) to higher (darker blue color) child food insecurity.
Participation Gaps Across States
Even when programs exist to help, not everyone who qualifies takes part. This chart shows the gap between those eligible for food aid and those actually participating, state by state. Each line connects 100% eligibility (gray) to the actual participation rate (green).

The story becomes clear: while some states manage to reach most of their eligible residents, others leave many behind. Look closely and you'll see where outreach is working, and where there's room for improvement. This is more than numbers; it's about real people who could benefit from support but aren't receiving it.
Too Many Kids Arrive at School Hungry
Each circle represents a group of children, with green showing those who eat a free or reduced-price breakfast and red showing those who don't. Out of the 21 million eligible children, only about half participate. Hungry kids can't focus, and missing breakfast has real consequences for learning, attention, and overall well-being. This visualization highlights the urgent need to make sure every child starts the day nourished.
Income Makes All the Difference
When examining food insecurity by focusing on income, a stark divide emerges. Families living below 185% of the federal poverty level face higher rates of childhood food insecurity compared to those above this threshold. This income line, often used to determine eligibility for federal assistance programs, reveals just how much economic stability shields children from hunger.

Explore the map below to see how this disparity plays out across every state in America. Toggle between income levels to witness the gap firsthand. In some states, children in low-income households are three to four times more likely to experience food insecurity than their higher-income peers.

This map shows the proportion of children (under 18) living in food-insecure households in 2023.

It includes both low and very low food insecurity which by the USDA definition means: low food security: reports of reduced quality, variety, or desirability of diet and little or no indication of reduced food intake and very low food security: reports of multiple indications of disrupted eating patterns and reduced food intake.
