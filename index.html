Beyond the Forecast

What environmental factors make Seattle weather feel hard for campus life?

Seattle weather is not just rain.

A standard forecast gives us useful numbers: temperature, precipitation, cloud cover, wind speed, sunrise time, and sunset time. But those numbers do not always explain how a day actually feels when students walk across campus, wait for buses, attend evening classes, or move between indoor and outdoor spaces.

This interactive article explores the gap between forecast numbers and lived campus experience. Instead of creating one universal weather difficulty score, we show the environmental layers that shape a Seattle campus day: rain, cloud cover, daylight, solar energy, wind, and temperature comfort.

Beyond the Forecast
Seattle weather is often described as “rainy,” but rain alone does not explain why some campus days feel harder than others.

This project asks: what environmental factors make Seattle weather feel hard?
What the Forecast Shows
A standard forecast gives us separate numbers: temperature, precipitation, cloud cover, wind speed, sunrise time, and sunset time. These numbers are useful, but they do not always show how the day actually feels when students move through campus.

This section breaks the forecast into visible weather layers instead of combining everything into one score.
Factor Patterns Across the Year
Instead of creating one weather difficulty index, users can explore each factor separately.

Select rain, cloud cover, daylight, solar energy, wind, or temperature comfort to see how that factor changes across the year.
Compare Factors Within a Month
A month can feel difficult for different reasons.

This view compares all six environmental factors within one selected month, helping users see that Seattle weather is layered, not just rainy.
Build Your Weekly Weather Lens
Users can choose the factors that matter most to their own routine.

A student who walks across campus may care more about rain and wind, while a student with evening classes may care more about daylight. When selected factors overlap on the same day, the day becomes visually darker.
A Day in Context
After selecting weather layers, users can inspect one day in detail.

This section shows the raw values behind a day: rain, cloud cover, daylight, solar energy, wind, and feels-like temperature. The goal is to explain the day through visible conditions, not through a hidden score.
Takeaway
This project does not claim that there is one universal measurement for a hard weather day.

Instead, it shows how different environmental factors appear, overlap, and matter differently depending on a student’s routine.

Seattle weather is not just rain. It is layered, contextual, and personal.

Final Takeaway

The main goal of this project is to help users move beyond a simple “Seattle is rainy” explanation. By separating and reconnecting different weather layers, the article helps students understand why some days feel harder than the forecast suggests.

Instead of hiding the story inside one score, the visualization keeps the factors visible: rain, cloud cover, daylight, solar energy, wind, and temperature comfort. Users can explore how each one changes across time and how different combinations may shape their own campus experience.
